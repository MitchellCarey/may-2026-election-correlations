The companion page to Winners asked which Census variables predict where each party won. This page asks where each party gained. Of the 213 wards with both a declared 2026 winner and a known prior party, 152 changed hands — Reform took 104, the Greens 27. The Census signal sharpens: flipped-to-Reform tracks +0.64 with apprenticeships and −0.50 with degree-holding — essentially the same fingerprint as the Winners page, because Reform's whole map is new. Flipped-to-Green swaps the polarity: +0.67 with under-29s, +0.65 with private renters, +0.59 with density — gains in the dense, young, renter-heavy inner cores. Smaller parties (LibDem 5 flips, Other 6, Labour 3, Conservative 3, Independent 4) sit too close to the noise floor to read off correlations cleanly; LibDem and Other are shown for completeness but flagged.

Method: "Prior party" is the top-of-poll party the last time each seat was contested before 7 May 2026 — for the nine thirds boroughs that's the May 2022 election (the same seat slot, four years apart); for Salford (all-out cycle) it's May 2021. Prior winners scraped from the per-borough Wikipedia articles for those years; see scripts/01b_extract_prior.py. Where a 2026 ward has no clean 1:1 prior because of the 2023/2024 boundary reviews in Bolton, Stockport, Trafford, and Wigan, we use the closest-predecessor ward (~31 wards, flagged ≈ in the appendix). One ward (Manchester · Miles Platting & Newton Heath) is still pending and is excluded from the analysis; Bury · Moorside (cancelled, rerun expected June) appears with no 2026 winner. Per-party correlation cards below show only parties with at least 5 flipped wards (MIN_FLIPS = 5); Pearson r on smaller samples is essentially noise.
