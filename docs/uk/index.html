Across the 2,433 declared wards in every Great Britain council that elected in 2026, Reform vs % apprenticeship sits at r = +0.46 — still the strongest single correlation in the dataset, though softer than the GM-only +0.64 once Tyne & Wear, the West Midlands metal towns and the Essex/Kent fringe are folded in. Reform's mean ward has 6.3% apprenticeship-trained residents vs 4.0% in Green wards. Reform also tracks negatively with degree-holding (−0.43), working-from-home (−0.36), SOC 1-3 graduate occupations (−0.35), private renting (−0.31) and density (−0.26); positively with UK-born share (+0.41) and no-qualifications (+0.31). Greens win the dense, young, privately-rented inner cores — Hackney, Lewisham, Lambeth, Norwich, Manchester. LibDems sweep the high-degree commuter belts — South Cambridgeshire, Sutton, Richmond, St Albans, Cheltenham, Stockport. Conservatives hold the affluent suburban fringe — Harrow, Bromley, Wandsworth, Kensington & Chelsea, Cannock Chase. Reform takes the post-industrial periphery, with full or near-full council sweeps in Hartlepool (12/12), Wigan (24/25), Wakefield (20/21), Tameside (18/19), Dudley (21/24) and Sunderland (19/25).

§ 4 Per-borough breakdown 2,434 wards · 2,433 declared

Reform won 646 of 2,433 declared wards across Great Britain — taking outright control of Hartlepool (12/12), Wigan (24/25), Wakefield (20/21), Tameside (18/19), Rochford (12/13), Redditch (8/9) and Dudley (21/24), and the largest single party in Sunderland (19/25), Halton (15/18), Plymouth (14/19), South Tyneside (14/18), Basildon (11/14) and Havant (9/12). Labour (532 wards) held inner-London — Greenwich (18/22), Redbridge (17/22), Ealing (16/24), Hammersmith & Fulham (16/21), Barking & Dagenham (14/19). Conservatives (417) held the affluent London fringe — Harrow (16/22), Bromley (14/22), Wandsworth (12/22), Kensington & Chelsea (12/18), Cannock Chase (12/12) — and clusters in the Home Counties. Method. 128 Great Britain councils held local elections on 7 May 2026; ward-level results were collected from each council's official results page where available, and from Wikipedia per-ward {{Election box winning candidate}} rolls where the council had not yet published a machine-readable result file. For each ward, the structural profile is built from ONS Census 2021 ward-level data: TS006 (density), TS007 (single-year age), TS063 (10-category occupation), TS067 (8-category qualifications, ages 16+), TS054 (9-category tenure), TS004 (12-category country of birth), TS061 (12-category travel to work), TS008 (sex), TS022 (ethnic group, detailed). Aggregations: % apprenticeship and % L4+ are share of residents 16+ (TS067 codes 3 and 5 respectively); % owned = TS054 codes 0+1+2; % social rent = codes 3+4; % private rent = codes 5+6+7 (rent-free bundled with private); % UK-born = TS004 code 1 / total; % WFH = TS061 code 1 / sum of codes 1-11 (employed denominator); % female = TS008 Female / (Female + Male); the five TS022 shares (% White, Asian, Black, Mixed, Other ethnic) are each top-level group / TS022 "All usual residents", with % Black combining the African and Caribbean sub-groups per the standard ONS 5-category presentation. Correlations are Pearson r vs a 0/1 party-win indicator across the full 2,433-ward declared sample.

Boundary mismatches. Bolton (2023 review), Stockport (2023 review), and Trafford (2023 review) all had ward boundary changes between Census 2021 and the 2026 election. Where 2026 ward names match 2021 ward boundaries, an exact GSS code lookup is used (104 of 161 new wards). Where names changed but boundaries are substantially the same, a fuzzy mapping is applied (33 wards) — e.g. Bolton's "Heaton, Lostock & Chew Moor" maps to 2021's "Heaton and Lostock". Where 2026 wards are splits or significant re-bounds of 2021 wards, an approximate proxy is used (e.g. Bolton's "Farnworth North" and "Farnworth South" both inherit the old "Farnworth" Census profile). This introduces some imprecision for these three boroughs, particularly Trafford, but doesn't materially affect the borough-level patterns. Wigan also had a 2024 boundary change handled the same way.

Coverage notes. 2,433 of 2,434 wards are declared. Manchester's Miles Platting & Newton Heath was still pending at the time of writing — the only undeclared ward across all 128 GB councils. Bury's Moorside ward election was cancelled in April 2026 after Reform candidate Victor Hagan died (rerun expected June 2026) and is not counted here. Multi-seat wards (where two or more seats were up in 2026) are counted once each by winning party. Per-ward turnout publication varies widely by council; this dataset focuses on winning party, vote share, and structural variables — turnout is preserved where the council published it.

Caveats. Independent (n=91) and Other (n=46) categories combine heterogeneous local parties (residents' associations, Workers Party of Britain, community labels, plus the long tail of single-ward minor parties) so their correlation profiles are weak by construction. The five main-party samples are all substantial (Reform n=646, Labour n=532, Conservative n=417, LibDem n=355, Green n=346), so r values across the GB dataset are statistically tight. Reform's structural correlation versus % apprenticeship is +0.46 GB-wide — softer than the GM-only +0.64, because the GB sample folds in geographies (London-fringe, the Home Counties, the South-East coast) where the apprenticeship/degree axis isn't as cleanly aligned with the Reform vote as it is across the post-industrial North.
