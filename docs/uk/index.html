Across the 213 declared wards in every Great Britain council that elected in 2026, Reform vs % apprenticeship sits at r = +0.64 — the single strongest correlation in the dataset. Reform's mean ward has 6.8% apprenticeship-trained residents vs 3.2% in Green wards. Reform also tracks negatively with degree-holding (−0.50), working-from-home (−0.45) and density (−0.36), and positively with UK-born share (+0.46). Conservative and LibDem wards share the highest-degree, highest-WFH profiles — Conservatives in lower-density rural-fringe seats (Bowdon, Hale, Bamford, Norden), LibDems in dense suburban knowledge-worker belts (Stockport, Timperley). Greens win the dense, young, privately-rented inner cores. Reform sweeps the post-industrial periphery.

Method. All 10 Great Britain borough councils held local elections on 7 May 2026; ward-level results were collected from each council's official results page or the most authoritative local source (bolton.gov.uk, oldham.gov.uk, stockport.gov.uk, trafford.gov.uk, electionresults.wigan.gov.uk for council-direct; Bury Times via Yahoo, Tameside Correspondent, Rochdale Times, Salford Now, salfordmedia.co.uk, Northern Quota for the rest). For each ward, the structural profile is built from ONS Census 2021 ward-level data: TS006 (density), TS007 (single-year age), TS063 (10-category occupation), TS067 (8-category qualifications, ages 16+), TS054 (9-category tenure), TS004 (12-category country of birth), TS061 (12-category travel to work), TS008 (sex). Aggregations: % apprenticeship and % L4+ are share of residents 16+ (TS067 codes 3 and 5 respectively); % owned = TS054 codes 0+1+2; % social rent = codes 3+4; % private rent = codes 5+6+7 (rent-free bundled with private); % UK-born = TS004 code 1 / total; % WFH = TS061 code 1 / sum of codes 1-11 (employed denominator); % female = TS008 Female / (Female + Male). Correlations are Pearson r vs a 0/1 party-win indicator across the full 213-ward declared sample.

Boundary mismatches. Bolton (2023 review), Stockport (2023 review), and Trafford (2023 review) all had ward boundary changes between Census 2021 and the 2026 election. Where 2026 ward names match 2021 ward boundaries, an exact GSS code lookup is used (104 of 161 new wards). Where names changed but boundaries are substantially the same, a fuzzy mapping is applied (33 wards) — e.g. Bolton's "Heaton, Lostock & Chew Moor" maps to 2021's "Heaton and Lostock". Where 2026 wards are splits or significant re-bounds of 2021 wards, an approximate proxy is used (e.g. Bolton's "Farnworth North" and "Farnworth South" both inherit the old "Farnworth" Census profile). This introduces some imprecision for these three boroughs, particularly Trafford, but doesn't materially affect the borough-level patterns. Wigan also had a 2024 boundary change handled the same way.

Coverage notes. 213 of 214 wards are declared. Manchester's Miles Platting & Newton Heath was still pending at the time of writing. Bury's Moorside ward election was cancelled in April 2026 after Reform candidate Victor Hagan died; rerun expected June 2026. Salford's Cadishead & Lower Irlam had two seats up (both Reform); counted once. Bury count assumes a 16-of-17 declared sample. Per-ward turnout is published for Tameside, Trafford, Oldham, Rochdale, Bury (45% borough-wide), Stockport (44% borough-wide), and one Salford ward (Swinton Park 39.45%); other boroughs published only borough-level turnouts. Council Tax Support data is Manchester-only — other boroughs run separate Council Tax Reduction schemes that aren't directly comparable.

Caveats. Independent and Other categories combine heterogeneous local parties (Horwich & Blackrod First, Radcliffe First, The Oldham Group Party, Edgeley Community Association, Workers Party of Britain, etc.) so their correlation profiles are weak by construction. The Conservative sample (n=11) and LibDem sample (n=19) are smaller than Reform (n=104) so their correlations are wider; the means in §4 are more reliable than the per-variable r values. Reform's structural correlation (vs % apprenticeship +0.64, vs L4+ −0.50) weakened slightly from the 48-ward Manchester+Salford sample (+0.75 / −0.50) but remains the strongest single signal in the dataset.
